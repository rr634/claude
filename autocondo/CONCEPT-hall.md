# 8 AT RIO: covered-hall refinement

Notes for the design team on turning the open courtyard into one covered, drive-in hall. Figures are
preliminary estimates taken from the DRC concept sheet (Sept. 2026) and need to be confirmed.

## The move

The buildable area is 105′ wide (125′ lot less 10′ side setbacks). Two 40′-deep rows leave a **25′ gap**,
and a 24′ aisle is the standard for 90° pull-in parking. The courtyard is already sized to be a drive aisle,
so the change is to roof it, enclose it and drive into it.

- One overhead glass door on SE 12th Street. It's the only vehicle entry.
- A 25′ × 90′ conditioned hall with a skylight spine.
- Eight glass sectional garage doors facing the hall.
- A turntable (about 18′ diameter) at the north end, so cars leave nose-first and nobody reverses 90′.
- A small detail bay and owners' lounge at the north end, plus a glass end wall onto the pool terrace,
  which becomes the only open-air amenity.
- Most of the courtyard landscaping goes. Planting stays on the 10′ side yards, the front yard and the
  pool terrace.

## Eight identical bays

90′ of building length divided by four bays gives 22′-6″ per bay, so all eight can be the same size:
22′-6″ × 40′, about 900 SF plus mezzanine. That replaces the current 6 × 880 SF + 2 × 960 SF. The total
footprint is unchanged, and so is the 7,200 SF of unit area. Doors line up across the hall.

## Questions for the architect, code consultant and City

1. **RML-25 limits:** check the maximum lot coverage, the minimum open space or landscaped area, and the
   FAR. Do any of them cap the hall? If coverage is the problem, a roofed but open-sided hall with a canopy
   or operable louvers may count differently.
2. **Enclosed parking garage:** an enclosed drive aisle probably triggers parking-garage provisions (FBC and
   NFPA 88A / NFPA 30A). That likely means mechanical exhaust with CO/NO₂ detection, sprinklers in the hall
   and the bays, and rated separation between each bay and the hall.
3. **Egress and light:** each residence needs an exit and light that don't depend on the hall. Plan exterior
   doors and windows on the 10′ side-yard walls, plus clerestory or skylight light on the mezzanines.
4. **Fire access:** check hose-pull distances from SE 12th Street to the far end of the hall, and the
   width and clearance of the gate.
5. **Driveway and curb cut:** the DRC sheet shows a pedestrian entry. A vehicle entry needs a curb cut,
   a driveway apron in the 25′ front yard, and a queue for the gate.
6. **Street face:** in Rio Vista, the DRC will look at how a single 105′ enclosed frontage reads from the
   street. The lit monument walls and a glass gate help.
7. **Pool and cabana in the 20′ rear yard:** confirm accessory-structure setbacks.
8. **Cost:** the hall adds roof area, skylight, HVAC and ventilation, and the turntable. Weigh that against
   the price premium for drive-in, fully enclosed bays.

## Fallback if the City pushes back

Keep everything above, but make the hall **covered and open-sided**: a solid or louvered roof with screened
ends and no conditioning. Owners still drive in under cover and the doors still face each other. Coverage,
FAR and ventilation become much easier to satisfy.
