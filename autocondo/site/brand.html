The Mark

The 8 is
the building.

The numeral is drawn as the section through a residence. The larger lower loop is the 14-foot garage, the smaller upper loop is the 8-foot mezzanine, and the gold line between them is the mezzanine slab. Together they make the 22-foot building height, and they read as an 8.

14′Lower loop: the garage and living level, where the car lives
8′Upper loop: the mezzanine lounge
22′The whole mark: the building height
—The slab, always in Glow. It's the one warm line in the mark.

Clear space

Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the upper loop of the 8 on every side. Nothing else enters that zone.

Minimum size

Horizontal lockup: 24px or 0.3″ tall. Monogram: 16px. Below that, use the favicon version, which has a heavier stroke.

Don't

Recolor the slab. It's always Glow, or the same color as the strokes in one-color printing.

Don't

Fill the loops, add shadows or outlines, or set "AT RIO" in a typeface.

Don't

Stretch, rotate or re-space the letters. Use the supplied SVG files.

Don't

Place it on a busy part of a photo. Darken the image first or use a solid panel.

Color

Dark rooms.
Warm light.

The palette comes from the place itself: a black ceiling, a limestone monument wall and the warm glow of the landscape lights. Keep surfaces about 80% dark, 15% limestone and no more than 5% Glow.

Graphic Devices

Three moves.
Use them sparingly.

These devices come straight from the space: the warm line of the slab, light streaking across a polished floor, and the number on each door.

The Slab Line

A single 1–2px Glow rule. Use it under eyebrows, as a divider and in the mark. Never more than one per view.

Floor Streaks

Soft horizontal light lines, like linear LEDs reflected in epoxy. Use them as background texture on dark panels.

01040508

Bay Numbers

Two digits, always zero-padded and set in wide Archivo. Use them on doors, keys, plans and owner materials.

Voice

Say less.
Say it true.

Talk like a collector rather than a brochure. Keep sentences short, use real numbers and skip the superlatives. Because this is a pre-construction condominium in Florida, accuracy is part of the brand.

Do

  • Lead with the car: the drive-in garage, the 14-foot ceiling, the mezzanine view.
  • Express exclusivity as scarcity: "only eight."
  • Mark figures as approximate and say where they came from ("per conceptual site plan").
  • Label renderings "conceptual" and materials as "design direction."
  • Keep the §718.503 statement and the Chapter 718 no-offer language on all advertising.

Don't

  • Imply buyers are screened or chosen. Avoid phrases like "by application" or "members approved." That risks fair-housing problems.
  • Promise lifts, finishes, appliances or amenities as fact before the documents say so.
  • Use photos of other buildings as if they were 8 AT RIO.
  • Say "for sale," "reserve now" or quote prices before the required filings are made.
  • Use "luxury," "world-class" or "unparalleled."
