Is Home Title Lock worth it? Honest alternatives

DeedGuard USA · Reviewed by a Florida real estate attorney · Updated November 2026

Home Title Lock is a real monitoring service (about $20/month) that alerts you to documents recorded against your property and offers a capped "restoration" services credit if fraud occurs. Its headline "$1 million protection" is not insurance and not cash — it's a spending limit on restoration help, delivered by a call center rather than attorneys. Cheaper options (free county alerts) and more complete ones (attorney-backed monitoring) exist.

We build a competing product, so weigh this accordingly — but every claim here is based on Home Title Lock's own public materials, and we link you to free options we don't profit from.

What Home Title Lock actually gives you

  • Title monitoring of public records for your property.
  • Alerts when a document is recorded.
  • Restoration help — a U.S.-based team, with "up to $1 million" in restoration services.

Pricing is roughly $19.95/month, or about $17.95/month on a longer prepaid term.

The catch (what the ads don't emphasize)

  • "$1M protection" is a capped service credit, not insurance. It's a limit on restoration assistance — not a payout you receive.
  • It's a call center, not attorneys. When something goes wrong, you get a restoration team, not a licensed real estate lawyer.
  • Monitoring is something you can partly do free. Many counties offer free recording alerts (see below).
  • Marketing leans on fear, and online reviews are heavily company-posted.

Free: check if your county offers recording alerts

Before paying anyone, see whether your county Clerk or Recorder offers a free property-fraud alert sign-up — many Florida counties do. It's basic (just an email when something is filed), but it's free. The trade-off: you still have to understand the alert and find help fast if it's real.

When a paid service is worth it

Pay for a service when you want (a) alerts you'll actually understand, and (b) someone qualified to help the moment fraud appears. That's where an attorney-backed option pulls ahead of a call-center model — you're not left decoding a scary notice alone.
