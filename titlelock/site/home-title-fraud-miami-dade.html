Home title fraud in Miami-Dade County

DeedGuard USA · Reviewed by a Florida real estate attorney · Updated November 2026

In Miami-Dade County, deeds are recorded with the Clerk of the Court's Official Records. To check your title, search the Official Records by your name or the property's legal description. To protect it, monitor for any new filing and act within days if something appears. DeedGuard monitors Miami-Dade records for you, with a Florida real estate attorney on call.

Why Miami-Dade is a hotspot

Miami-Dade combines everything fraudsters look for: a huge stock of condos and vacant lots, many out-of-state and international owners, seasonal residents who are away for months, and high property values that mean more equity to steal. That makes home title fraud a real risk here — especially for second homes and investment properties.

How to check your deed in Miami-Dade

  1. Go to the Miami-Dade Clerk of the Court Official Records search.
  2. Search by your name (grantor/grantee) or the property's legal description or folio.
  3. Review every document recorded against the property — deeds, mortgages, liens, judgments — and confirm nothing was filed that you didn't authorize.

It's smart to do this periodically. The limitation: a one-time check only tells you about the past — fraud can be recorded the day after you look, which is why ongoing monitoring matters.

Free: Miami-Dade owner alerts

Check whether the Miami-Dade Clerk currently offers a free Owner Alert / property-fraud notification that emails you when a document is recorded under your name. It's free and worth enabling — just understand it's a basic alert, with no explanation of what the filing means and no one to help you respond.

If you find a fraudulent filing

  • Contact a Florida real estate attorney immediately.
  • Notify the Miami-Dade Clerk and local law enforcement.
  • Begin a quiet-title action to restore clean title.
  • Act fast — delay lets the fraudster borrow or sell against your home.
