How to protect your home's title in Florida

DeedGuard USA · Reviewed by a Florida real estate attorney · Updated November 2026

To protect your Florida home's title: (1) monitor your county's public records for new filings, (2) act within days if anything appears, (3) add the ALTA 49/49.1 forgery endorsement where it's available, and (4) keep a real estate attorney on call. You can do the monitoring yourself through free county alerts, or have a service like DeedGuard do steps 1, 2 and 4 for you.

Why Florida homeowners are especially exposed

Florida is one of the most-targeted states for home title theft. It has millions of second homes, snowbird and vacation properties, out-of-state and foreign owners, and large numbers of vacant lots and condos — exactly the homes nobody is actively watching. See what home title fraud is and who gets targeted.

Step 1 — Monitor the public record

Ownership changes show up as new recordings at your county. Watch for new deeds, mortgages, liens, and judgments on your property. Two ways to do it:

  • Free: sign up for your county Clerk/Recorder's property-fraud alert, if offered (many Florida counties have one). You'll get an email when something is filed under your name.
  • Paid/automated: a monitoring service re-scans the record for you, scores the risk, and explains what a filing means — so you're not left decoding a county notice alone.

Step 2 — Act fast if something appears

A fraudulent filing is far easier to unwind early. If you spot one: contact a Florida real estate attorney immediately, notify the county recorder and law enforcement, and begin a quiet-title action to restore clean title. Days matter — the longer a forged deed sits, the more a fraudster can borrow or sell against it.

Step 3 — Add forgery coverage where available

The owner's title policy you bought at closing generally doesn't cover forgery that happens after closing (Exclusion 3(d)). The newer ALTA 49 / 49.1 endorsements add post-closing forgery coverage to an owner's policy where they're sold through a licensed title agent or underwriter. It's optional and separate from monitoring — but worth asking about.

Step 4 — Keep a real estate attorney on call

The difference between a scare and a disaster is often how fast you get qualified help. Having a Florida real estate attorney already in your corner — rather than a call center — means a suspicious filing gets handled correctly and quickly.
